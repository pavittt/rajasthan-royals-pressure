What was searched: every situation, matchup and phase

Every pressure moment was searched for, not chosen by hand. Each candidate is an over band plus up to two details, tested in every phase:

  • Phases and over bands: powerplay (overs 1–6), middle overs (7–10, 11–15, 7–15), overs 16–18, the last two overs (19–20) and the death as a whole (16–20).
  • Batter: batting position (openers, No. 3–4, No. 5–7, No. 8–11) and hand (right- or left-handed).
  • Bowler type, six kinds: right-arm pace, left-arm pace, off-spin, left-arm finger spin, leg-spin and left-arm wrist spin; and normal ball or wrong'un.
  • Wickets and collapse: wickets down (0–1, 2–3, 4–5, 6+), early wickets in the powerplay, a collapse in the middle overs, a new batter (first 6 balls) and a set partnership.
  • Match state: a close game (win chance 30–70%) or one tilted to one side, batting first or chasing, required rate (chasing 10+ an over), three dot balls in a row, and a bowler just hit for 20+.

That gave 1,169 narrow situations. On top of them the 588-cell audit crossed batting role × right/left hand × the six bowler types × the three phases, plus thirteen pressure situations × bowler type and × batting role. A situation became one of RR's 48 moments only if the champions beat the league (luck chance under 10% against 1,000 random "champions"), RR 2025–26 fell short (below the league in at least 85% of 1,000 resamples of RR's matches) and plain numbers agreed. A decision-tree search for deeper three-way splits found none that beat 300 shuffles, so moments stop at two details.

1. What a pressure moment is

Every spot in a match was tested, in every phase. A spot is an over band plus up to two details:

  • batting position, the batter's hand, or bowler type (right- or left-arm pace, off-spin, left-arm finger spin, leg-spin, left-arm wrist spin);
  • normal ball or wrong'un, wickets down, or a new batter (first 6 balls);
  • match state: a close game (win chance 30–70%) or one tilted to one side, batting first or chasing, or a bowler just hit for 20+.

The full grid of 588 role × hand × bowler type × phase cells was checked too. A spot became a pressure moment only if it passed all four tests:

  • Champions win it: the five IPL winners 2022–26 beat the league there, with under a 10% chance of luck (1,000 random "champions").
  • RR lose it: RR 2025–26 are at least 0.05 wins a season below the league.
  • It's real: RR were below the league in at least 85% of 1,000 resamples of their matches.
  • Plain numbers agree: strike rate, economy or wickets point the same way.

48 spots passed, 24 with the bat and 24 with the ball. In wins a season lost: batting in the last two overs 1.54, batting in the middle overs 0.56, bowling at the death 0.41, bowling in the middle overs 0.32 and the new ball 0.26.

4. Why the top 10 can be trusted

An independent review found four problems in the earlier scoring, and all four are fixed:

  • The same ball was counted in several moments. Now each ball counts once.
  • The IPL and 2026 weights did not reduce T20I-only records. Now they reduce the evidence itself.
  • Players were not compared with their own usual level. Now D does this.
  • Missing data was scored as average. Now such players are left out.

The 48 moments themselves are unchanged.

  • Current form: every pick played 5+ IPL matches in 2026 and is not below the league under pressure in 2026. This removed Pathirana, Shardul Thakur, Dewald Brevis, Starc and Hetmyer.
  • Resampling matches 1,000 times: about 7 of the 10 batters and 7 of the 10 bowlers stay in the top 10. The top three or four on each side are firm.
  • 22 pre-declared alternatives: one setting is changed at a time, such as shrinkage, IPL-only, 2025 weighted equal to 2026, the minimum sample or the form rule. Most keep 8–10 of the 10.
  • Moments that hold up in both seasons: using only those keeps 7 of 10 batters and 8 of 10 bowlers.
  • Placebo test: with the same number of "pressure" balls picked at random from each player's balls, only about 3 of the 10 batters and 4 of the 10 bowlers survive, so the lists come from RR's pressure moments, not from general quality.
  • Honest limits: split-half repeatability of the final score is near zero (−0.10 batting, 0.07 bowling), while 2025-only and 2026-only rankings agree moderately (0.33 and 0.57). The order below about 4th is uncertain. The data has no birth dates, so age is not modelled.

Deeper three-way splits: model search

Step 2

Final top 10s on RR's 48 moments

Pressure is the same 48 RR moments from step 1, unchanged. Only how players are scored on them was fixed:

  • Each ball is counted once, even when it sits in several overlapping moments. A ball in a bigger RR gap counts more: its weight is scaled to average 1 and capped at 3.
  • Live balls only. Within the moments, a ball counts when the result can still swing: its leverage is at or above the median of all balls. Batting pressure balls swing the result about 2.5 times as much as other balls, and bowling pressure balls about 3.3 times.
  • Each player is compared with the league and with himself. Score = P + D. P is his run value per pressure ball. D is pressure minus his own usual level. Both are in runs per 100 balls above league expectation and shrunk for small samples. The same formula is used for batters and bowlers.
  • IPL first, recent first. As evidence, an IPL 2026 ball counts 1.0, IPL 2025 0.7, a 2026 T20I between the top nine nations 0.5, and a 2025 one 0.35. A T20I-heavy record really does count for less. Nothing before 2025 counts.
  • Eligibility, fixed in advance: played in 2026; at least 40 effective pressure balls, 20 of them in IPL; bowlers average 2+ overs a match. A player without enough data is left out, never counted as average.
  • Current form, fixed in advance: 5+ IPL matches in 2026, and not below the league under pressure in 2026 (more than half a standard error below is out). That leaves 32 batters and 38 bowlers.

How the numbers were made

Every ball's chance of winning comes from a win-probability model trained on the supplied ball-by-ball data. A situation's value for a team-season is the average change in that chance per ball, compared with the league in the same season. The luck chance is the share of 1,000 draws in which a random team from each season did as well as the real champions. RR's shortfall was checked by resampling RR's 2025–26 matches; every moment shown was below the league in at least 85% of resamples. Role checks use all IPL matches 2022–26. Champions are GT (2022), CSK (2023), KKR (2024) and RCB (2025, 2026).
