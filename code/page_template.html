      The method in four steps

      1

      Find the moments

      1,169 narrow situations were tested in every phase: an over band plus up to two of batting position, batter hand, bowler type, delivery, wickets down, required rate, new batter, set partnership and match closeness. A situation became a moment only if the champions beat the league (luck chance under 10% against 1,000 random "champions") and RR 2025–26 fell short (below the league in at least 85% of 1,000 resamples of RR's matches), with plain numbers agreeing. After removing duplicates: 48 moments, 24 batting and 24 bowling.

      2

      Keep live balls, weight the big leaks

      Within the moments, a ball counts as pressure only when the result can still swing: its leverage (expected change in win probability, from an out-of-fold win-probability model) is at or above the median. Each ball counts once. A ball in a bigger RR gap counts more (scaled to average 1, capped at 3×).

      3

      Rate the players

      Each ball is valued in runs above the league expectation for the same format, year, innings, over, wickets and required rate. Wickets are priced by win-probability cost, and each ball is adjusted for the opposition. P = value per pressure ball. U = value per ordinary ball. D = P − U. Score = P + D, both shrunk by empirical Bayes for small samples. The same formula is used for batters and bowlers.

      4

      Fair and current

      Evidence weights: IPL 2026 1.0, IPL 2025 0.7, T20Is between the top nine nations 0.5 (2026) and 0.35 (2025). Nothing before 2025 counts. Eligibility, fixed before ranking: 40+ effective pressure balls (20+ IPL); bowlers 2+ overs a match; 5+ IPL matches in 2026; and not below the league under pressure in 2026 (more than half a standard error below is out). No player is counted as average for missing data.

      Reliability and stability

      Split-half: the matches were split in two at random 100 times and each half's scores compared. With only two seasons and narrow pressure moments, the final score barely repeats between random halves of matches (near zero for both sides), while the 2025-only and 2026-only rankings agree moderately. This is the main limit of the method: who is in the lists is firmer than the order inside them, as the rank ranges show.

      Pre-declared alternatives

      Each row changes one setting and re-runs the whole pipeline. Overlap is how many of the official top 10 stay in that alternative's top 10.

      Limits

      • The moments come from RR's own weak spots, so this ranks who wins the moments RR lose. A general rule (top 25% leverage balls) gives a very different batting list; it is reported in the alternatives table.
      • No single moment passes a strict false-discovery test on its own. The live-ball filter, shrinkage, the both-seasons check and the resampling guard against chance.
      • Only two seasons are scored, so split-half repeatability of the final score is low and ranks below about 4th are uncertain. The lists are more certain than the order within them.
      • The data has no birth dates, so age is not modelled. Current form is handled by the 2026 weighting and the current-form rule.
      • For bowlers, the pressure-minus-usual part (D) barely repeats, so it is shrunk to almost the same value for everyone and bowling ranks rest on pressure level (P).

      Reproducibility

      Every step is a numbered script that reads its inputs and writes its outputs (run_all.sh). All thresholds sit in config.py and were fixed before the rankings were read; the current-form rule and its two alternatives are in the sensitivity table. The code is supplied with this report.
